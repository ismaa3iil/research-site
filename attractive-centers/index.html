Attractive Centers

What happens to a mean of points when you pull one of its inputs? These three papers develop that question from scalar means to Euclidean geometry and the attainable locations of triangle centers. The papers are available as PDF downloads. Older versions

Paper 1 · Revised 19 November 2026

Attractive centers: regular means of points and a sharp window for Fréchet p-means

Introduces attractivity as a geometric counterpart to monotonicity for means of numbers, and proves the sharp dimension-independent interval of exponents for which the pointwise Fréchet mean is always attractive.

Paper 2 · Revised 19 November 2026

Attractive centers: where can a regular mean of three points lie?

Derives the standard triangle chest, shows with two vertex moves that it is not generally sharp, and distinguishes attainable locations from analytic attainment on the isosceles axis.

Download Paper 2 (PDF)

Paper 3 · November 2026

Attractive triangle centers: a common boundary for hearts and chests

For triangles under the wide motion convention, the intersection of all finite equivariant network chests equals the closure of the analytic Heart. Every relative-interior value is attained by an analytic rule; attainment of every boundary point is not asserted.

Interactive geometry explorer

The triangle explorer now shows the enlarged certified heart by default: B46, the constant and shape-dependent families, the repaired continuous conic bridge, R1–R10, P1–P3, and (17 November 2026) 300 interval-certified Steiner-frame maps. Compare the earlier hearts with checkboxes, drag the vertices, and read the formulas and proof status in the guide. Four planar points and tetrahedra retain their power hearts and geometric chest methods; since 18 November 2026 their hearts also include face means of the certified triangle centers, and their chests include pin rules and two-step cuts. An illustrated help page and three PDF documents explain every layer and algorithm. Light plots, equal coordinate scales, and live area and volume percentages support comparison.
